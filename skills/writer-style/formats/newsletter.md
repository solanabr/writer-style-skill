# Container: email newsletter

An issue of a recurring personal newsletter: an email FROM A PERSON the reader chose to let into
their inbox. The register is one-to-one ("I" expected, "you" is one reader), even though it goes
to thousands. Recurring trust is the asset — every issue either compounds it or spends it.

## Contract
- **Subject line ≤50 chars, concrete.** The best subject is the sharpest fact inside ("Anchor 1.0
  breaks every CPI call"), never clickbait the body doesn't cash, never "Newsletter #47".
- **Open with the one thing that matters this issue** — not "welcome back", not "it's been a busy
  week", not a table of contents. First line = why this email deserved the open.
- **Items connect where honest, stand alone where not.** A forced narrative thread through
  unrelated items reads as newsletter-workshop technique; "three things this week, unrelated,
  all useful" is a legitimate structure.
- **Per item: what happened → why you care → one action.** Mini-headers or bold lead-ins are
  fine; full H2 ceremony is not (it's an email, not a blog).
- **Links inline where the action is**; carry `[LINK]` placeholders verbatim.
- **Length 400–800 words.** Respect the inbox: cut the item that isn't earning its scroll.
- **Sign-offs are native here** — this is the one container where a personal sign-off is the
  norm rather than a marker spend. One, matching the pack's register.

## Reads as AI here (instant fails)
- "Welcome to another issue of…" / "In this issue:" TOC for a <800-word email.
- Uniform item geometry: three items, each exactly two paragraphs, each ending on a zinger.
- A "TL;DR" block atop a short email (either write short or don't).
- Curator-voice detachment ("Interesting piece on X worth your time") with no personal stake —
  the person's OWN take is why they're subscribed.
- Every item force-threaded into a theme ("speaking of speed…").

## Craft notes
- The strongest recurring move: one line of what the AUTHOR did/broke/learned this week — the
  seam that makes it an email from a person.
- The reply channel is real: a genuine "tell me if X" invites replies; a rhetorical one is bait.
