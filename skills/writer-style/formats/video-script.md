# Container: video script (short + long)

Two variants, one discipline: these words will be SAID, on camera, by the author. The read-aloud
test is not a check here — it is the medium. If a sentence can't be spoken in one breath without
rehearsal, split it.

**Short** (`video-script-short`): 60–90s vertical (Short/Reel), ~150–220 spoken words.
**Long** (`video-script-long`): talking-head explainer, ~140–160 spoken words per minute
(a 5-minute video is ~700–800 words).

## Contract (both variants)
- **Spoken register throughout**: contractions, fragments, restarts ("so the— okay, simplest
  version:") used sparingly and naturally. No subordinate-clause towers, no semicolons, nothing
  that only works on a page.
- **Directions in brackets, lean**: `[cut: terminal]`, `[b-roll: explorer page]`, `[beat]`,
  `[on-screen: 5,000 lamports]`. Directions are production notes, not prose; a script drowning
  in directions wasn't written to be said.
- **On-screen text ≠ spoken text**: numbers/identifiers the viewer must retain go on screen
  (bracketed) AND spoken rounded-honestly if awkward to say — the exact value must appear in one
  of the two channels verbatim.
- **One CTA maximum, at the close**, in-voice. Mid-video CTA breaks read corporate.

## Short variant
- **The hook IS the first line** — the claim or tension inside ~2 seconds, no channel intro, no
  "hey guys", no "in this video". First words on the wound: "you're signing for an address that
  has no private key" — then explain.
- One idea, deflated to its simplest true model. If a second idea appears, cut it.
- End on the payoff restated in one line, or a hard stop after the punch. No outro.

## Long variant
- **Cold open ≤20 seconds**: the felt problem, then the promise ("by the end you'll have X").
- Structure by beats, not headings: each beat a claim + proof (demo, number, drawing).
- The camera presence carries the seams — an honest aside lands better spoken than written; use
  the author's real ones (substance bank), never manufactured.
- Silence is punctuation: `[beat]` after the number that matters.

## Reads as AI here (instant fails)
- "Hey guys, welcome back to the channel" / "in today's video" — dead openers.
- Essay prose read aloud: written transitions ("Furthermore…"), page rhythm, zero breath points.
- Hype-cadence uniformity (every sentence an announcement) — spoken enthusiasm spikes and rests
  like the naturalness floor says.
- "Don't forget to like and subscribe" mid-video; triple CTAs.
- A script whose bracketed directions describe stock-footage clichés (handshake, city timelapse).
