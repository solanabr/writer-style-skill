# Container: LinkedIn post

150–300 words of connected prose. The feed shows roughly the first 200 characters before
"…see more" — the hook lives there or nowhere. The audience skews professional and includes
non-specialists; credibility here is calm competence, not feed-energy.

## Contract
- **Hook inside the visible preview**: the problem or the surprising number in sentence one, in
  plain professional language (jargon in line 1 filters out exactly the readers LinkedIn adds).
- **Real paragraphs, 1–3 sentences each.** Airier than a blog, denser than X.
- **NO broetry** — the one-sentence-per-line stack with dramatic
  line
  breaks
  is the platform's most-mocked AI/growth-hacker tell. Full stop.
- **Substance over story-template.** The "personal anecdote → pivot → business lesson → moral"
  arc is the #1 LinkedIn slop structure. A real story (from the pack's substance bank) told
  straight is fine; a story with a bolted-on moral is not. Never invent a story.
- **Hashtags: none, or ≤3 at the very end.** Operators with real audiences mostly use none.
- **Emoji sparing** (0–2); never as bullet decoration on every line.
- **Links**: carry the brief's `[LINK]` verbatim where the flow wants it. (Native operators often
  post links in comments for reach; noting "link in comments" is acceptable if the brief allows.)
- **Close with substance**: a verdict, an offer, a concrete next step. A genuine question is
  allowed only if the author actually wants the answers.

## Reads as AI here (instant fails)
- Broetry line breaks; "Agree?"; "Thoughts?"; "Let that sink in."
- "I'm humbled/thrilled/excited to announce" — corporate-milestone voice.
- The em-dash-studded aphorism cascade; three parallel sentences in a row.
- "Here's what X taught me about Y" where Y is business.
- Hashtag piles (#blockchain #web3 #innovation #future).
- A recap of the post inside the post ("The bottom line?").

## Craft notes
- LinkedIn rewards ONE precise claim a professional can repeat in a meeting. Give them the
  sentence they'll steal.
- Numbers carry extra weight with this audience — lead with the strongest one, styled honestly
  (approximations as approximations).
