# Calibration lessons — david pack (writer-facing, ≤10 lines, each cites its round; binding)

Round 1 = blind A/B on a 3.2k-word course opener (Superteam Academy, "Solana Hackathon Expert",
lesson 1, 2026-09-06). Arms: A = this pack (course-opener mode, primary-only), B = plain register with
no persona, same brief and frozen facts. **Owner picked B.** His words: "too much text in places where
it didn't need. I want a version where it seems like someone wrote it instead of seeming like it was
written without a person behind it." Forensics: A carried 11 meta-narration sentences, 24 first-person
"I" in 3.3k words, 14 sections (141 to 357 words each), and the qualification opener; B carried 5, 0,
11 (221 to 447 words) and opened straight on the do-element.

1. [R1-A/B] **Never narrate the lesson about itself.** No announcement stubs in the course container:
   "let me say what X is", "I will name them now so the headings make sense", "I am going to say this
   once here", "since I mentioned it twice", "here is the thing I want you to see first". The
   corpus's announcement stubs are a Medium-post move; in a lesson they read as padding without a
   person. Say the thing; do not announce that you are about to say it.
2. [R1-A/B] **Exemplar `01-opener-qualification` is standalone-post only.** A course lesson opens on
   the backward hook and the do-element, never on "if you are X and want Y, this lesson is for you".
   The router does not deal `01` in `course-lesson` or `course-opener` mode.
3. [R1-A/B] **Sections follow the material, not the outline.** A 3k-word lesson runs 8 to 11 H2s of
   250 to 450 words; 14 sections restate. One section per new element; merge the ones that exist only
   to introduce the next.
4. [R1-A/B] **First person is a receipt, not connective tissue.** In course modes, "I" appears only
   where he states a position, reports a thing he did, or hedges a number. Budget: at most 8 per 3k
   words (A spent 24, mostly as "let me" and "I will"). The corpus's 14 to 31 per 1k belongs to the
   800-word post, not the lesson.
5. [R1-A/B] **"Someone wrote it" comes from judgment, not from voice markers.** What won in B: plain
   verdicts on what matters and what to skip, concrete subjects, a hedge on every number that deserves
   one, the reader addressed directly. Seams and refrains did not make A feel more human; they made it
   longer. Keep the naturalness floor (uneven texture, one section that does not pull its weight) and
   drop the marker appetite in course modes.
6. [R1-A/B] **Cut restatement before cutting anything else.** When a section re-explains a term
   defined two sections up, or previews what a later section will do, delete it. The validator's word
   count is a floor on material, never a target to pad toward.
7. [R1-owner, carried from kaue LESSONS #4] **Never tease a scheduled next part.** Course lessons
   close by naming the actual next lesson's first action, or close plain.
