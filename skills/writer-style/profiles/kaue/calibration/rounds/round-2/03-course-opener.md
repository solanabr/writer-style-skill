# Welcome to Solana Development Fundamentals (the honest version)

Welcome — I'm genuinely glad you're here! This is lesson 0, the one before the six actual modules, so no code today. The course covers a lot of ground over the next 8 weeks, and before any of it I want to give you the honest version of what you're signing up for: what you'll build, what it costs (spoiler: nothing), and exactly where it gets hard. Courses love selling you the montage, I'd rather hand you the map with the rough terrain already marked on it.

Quick admin, because everyone asks: the course is free, fully online, and self-paced with weekly checkpoints, which means you set the pace and once a week we check in so you don't drift off alone. Plan on 5–7 hours a week, knowing some weeks you'll do more because you're having fun and some weeks less because life happens; that's what self-paced is for.

## The map: 6 modules, 8 weeks

1. **Solana core concepts:** accounts, transactions, and programs, the mental model everything else in the course hangs on.
2. **Rust for Solana:** only the Rust you need, trimmed to the slice Solana programs actually use.
3. **Anchor framework:** where real program development starts, and where the course begins to feel like building.
4. **Client development:** TypeScript and the wallet adapter, so actual humans can use what you build.
5. **Testing and security:** LiteSVM for tests, plus the common vulnerability classes you'll learn to spot.
6. **Capstone:** ship a full dApp, program plus frontend, to devnet.

By week 8 you will have deployed a working program to devnet and built a frontend that talks to it, and that's a portfolio piece, not a certificate: a live thing you can put in front of a hiring manager or a hackathon judge and say "I built this, it's on devnet, go poke it."

## The price of admission

All you need is comfort in one programming language, any language: if you can write a loop and a function in Python, JavaScript, Go, whatever, you're in, and it doesn't matter whether your corner is backend, frontend, data or game dev; the fundamentals transfer. No Rust required, no blockchain background either; module 1 assumes you've never touched a chain in your life.

And do you need money to deploy on a real network? Guess what: no. The tooling is free end to end, and devnet SOL (the play-money version of the real thing) comes straight out of faucets, which, if you've ever paid real gas just to test something, is honestly a godsend. You'll finish this course without spending a cent.

## Weeks 2 and 3: the wall

Now, the part most courses bury in the FAQ. The Rust stretch (weeks 2 and 3) is the hardest part of this course, and I'm telling you on day zero because that's where most dropouts happen. Week 1 feels great, everything new and a little magic; the wall shows up in the second and third week, when the borrow checker starts arguing with code you were sure was correct and you catch yourself wondering, somewhere around the third compiler error on the same line, whether you somehow forgot how to program. You didn't. Everyone hits this wall. It passes. Theory and practice are two very different things, and the only way through Rust is making your own mistakes in it — which is uncomfortable, and also exactly the point.

Two things to hold on to for that stretch. First, module 2 promises only the Rust you need, and it means it: enough to write Solana programs, skip the rest. Second, and I mean this: you're not doing it alone, because the weekly checkpoints exist precisely for the "I'm stuck and a little embarrassed about it" moments, and showing up confused is exactly how we'll get you through it together. Asking for help in week 3 isn't falling behind, it's the course working as designed. (The borrow checker still humbles me now and then, and I'm the one teaching this course.)
