# Welcome to Solana Development Fundamentals

You know the loop. One night you decide it's finally time to learn Solana, you open the docs, and within an hour you've been told you need Rust, a new mental model for state, and apparently a different brain. So the tab closes. The plan moves to the someday pile, again.

This course exists to kill the someday pile.

Solana Development Fundamentals is free, fully online, and self-paced with weekly checkpoints. The only prerequisite is comfort in some programming language: any language, honestly. Python, JavaScript, whatever you write today. No Rust. No blockchain background. Nothing to buy.

Quick confession about who's writing this: I didn't get into building through some grand plan. A teacher handed me an Arduino kit in 10th grade, back when I was dead set on becoming a musician, and that was that. Nobody gave me a map though, so I wandered for years, and a lot of that wandering was avoidable. This course is the map I wish someone had handed me.

## The map: 6 modules, 8 weeks

Here's the whole shape of it, no mystery:

1. **Solana core concepts:** accounts, transactions, programs. The mental model everything else hangs on.
2. **Rust for Solana:** only the Rust you need. The whole language can wait.
3. **Anchor:** the framework you'll actually write programs in.
4. **Client development:** TypeScript and the wallet adapter. The part your users touch.
5. **Testing and security:** LiteSVM for fast tests, plus the common vulnerability classes, so you learn to spot them before you ever ship one.
6. **Capstone:** a full dApp, program plus frontend, deployed to devnet.

Module 6 is the reason the other five exist (it's also the fun one).

Will this cost you money at any point? Nope. The course is free and the tooling is free: devnet SOL comes out of a faucet, so there is no step in these 8 weeks where your wallet is the blocker. The only thing you spend here is time.

And self-paced doesn't mean alone, the weekly checkpoints keep a rhythm: hit one a week and you land the capstone on schedule, slow down when life happens and the course waits for you. It bends to your calendar, not the other way around.

## The honest part

Now the part most course intros skip. The commitment is 5–7 hours a week, and weeks 2–3, the Rust stretch, are the hardest part of the whole thing. I'm not saying that to lower your expectations, I'm saying it because that's where most of the quitting happens. You'll be writing code that fights back, in a language that cares about things your current language lets you ignore, and progress will feel slower than you think it should.

Here's what I'd tell a friend at that point: theory and practice are two very different concepts, and the uncomfortable stretch is exactly where one turns into the other. Every real thing I know how to build came out of a phase like that: confusing, slower than planned, full of my own mistakes. The checkpoints exist precisely for this stretch. Nobody finishes week 3 on talent, they finish it on showing up.

The pattern I keep seeing in new builders: they don't fail out of a course like this, they abandon it at the first stretch that doesn't flatter them. A rough week is just a rough week.

And it pays off fast. Somewhere in module 3, Anchor takes the Rust you fought for and starts paying it back: the thing compiles, and you feel it click.

## What you walk away with

By week 8, if you do the work, you have a working program deployed to devnet and a frontend that talks to it. That's the outcome this whole course points at: a portfolio piece, not a certificate. A thing with an address, that you built, that a stranger can poke at. When someone asks what you can do, you just send the link.

And shipping one real thing changes how the whole space looks to you afterwards: other people's programs stop reading like magic and start reading like decisions someone made, decisions you could now argue with.

Zoom out for a second. Whatever pulled you here (the tech itself, a career move, the itch to finally build instead of watch), the total price of finding out is those hours, week after week. That's it. That's the whole bill.

So here's my ask: give it the full 8 weeks before you judge yourself, and when week 2 gets rough (it will), don't let it convince you the ceiling is low. It isn't.

Module 1 opens with accounts, transactions, and what a program even is on this chain. It's a genuinely elegant model once it clicks, and everything after gets easier for knowing it. Bring questions to the checkpoints; asking early beats struggling quietly, every time.
