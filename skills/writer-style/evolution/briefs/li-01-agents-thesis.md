## Brief

A LinkedIn post (~200–300 words): the structural argument that autonomous agents becoming economic
actors is a problem banking cannot solve and crypto rails already do. The audience is professional
and largely crypto-skeptical — the post wins on the KYC logic, not on crypto vocabulary. Separate
what's live from what's projection. No broetry line-break formatting; paragraph rules in
`formats/linkedin-post.md`.

## Fact-sheet (FROZEN — imported subset of testbed/briefs/08-thesis-ai-agents.md; do not re-derive)

- Banks and card networks require a legal person behind every account (KYC). An autonomous agent
  cannot pass KYC; it can only ride on a human's credentials with a human in the loop.
- A crypto wallet is a keypair. An agent can hold one, sign with it, and spend from it — live
  today, not a roadmap item.
- x402 (Coinbase, 2025): an HTTP-402-based payment protocol for machine-to-machine payments — pay
  per call in stablecoins, no account or subscription.
- MCP (Anthropic, open standard): how agents attach to tools; pay-per-call APIs pair naturally
  with per-request micropayments.
- Settlement rail numbers: ~400ms blocks, fees at fractions of a cent (base fee 5,000
  lamports/signature).
- Honest constraints to name: agent custody of keys is unsolved (a compromised agent is a
  compromised wallet); regulatory treatment is undefined; volume projections are speculation.
