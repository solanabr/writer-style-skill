# AI Tells & Humanization — 2026 Research Synthesis

Working reference for the writer-style skill: what reads as AI in 2026, what to lint, what to stop
linting, and per-platform norms. Synthesized 2026-08-20 from four research tracks — practitioner
(Hassid + humanizer skills), measurement (slop metrics, stylometry, detectors), mechanism (why models
slop), platform (channel norms). Low-confidence uncorroborated claims dropped; folk figures flagged.

Framing facts:
- Slop is caused by preference alignment (RLHF annotator taste bias), not training data — tells rotate
  with every model generation, so the lexicon must be versioned and re-derived, never static (Juzek &
  Ward COLING 2025; arXiv 2508.01930).
- Engaged readers detect AI text at ~87.6%; heavy LLM users near 90% (arXiv 2502.11614). The stakes are
  reader trust, not detector scores.
- Detector evasion is a non-goal: word-choice distributions survive paraphrase/translation (97.1%
  five-way model attribution, ICML 2025) and commercial detectors train adversarially against
  humanizers (Pangram DAMAGE). Optimize human-perceived tells + voice fidelity instead.
- Cluster doctrine (Wikipedia WP:AISIGNS): single words prove nothing — density and co-occurrence are
  the signal. Both "too polished" and "conspicuously scrubbed" now fail.

## 1. TL;DR — top 10 moves for this skill

1. **Lint sentence templates, not just words.** Port slop-score's 10 guarded stage-1 regexes for the
   negative-parallelism family ("it's not just X, it's Y" — alone 25% of EQ-Bench's slop metric;
   Pangram calls it the tic of 2025-era models) plus contrast-reframe templates ("That's not X.
   That's Y." / "Stop X. Start Y."), fragment-question pivots ("The catch?"), and colon reveals.
   Budget ~0-1 hits per piece; hard-fail negative parallelism only when clause 2 adds no checkable fact.
2. **Era-tier and version the lexicon.** Down-weight the 2023 tier (delve, tapestry, testament);
   up-weight the mid-2025+ core (emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing) and the Juzek-Ward 21
   focal inflections. Date-stamp entries, keep an append log, have voice-validator emit "candidate new
   tells," and force a recalibration round on any underlying-model change.
3. **Add grammar-level detectors that survive vocabulary swaps:** trailing participial tails
   (", highlighting..."), copula-avoidance ratio (serves as / stands as / boasts vs plain is/are),
   nominalization density vs the Kaue exemplar baseline, and a rule-of-three budget. LLMs run 2-5x the
   human participial-clause rate and 1.5-2x nominalizations (PNAS 2025).
4. **Make burstiness numeric.** Per ~150 words, ≥1 sentence of ≤6 words; no three consecutive sentences
   within ±5 words of each other's length; per-section sentence-length stdev floor; ≥1 sentence outside
   the 10-30-token band. (The circulating "25% vs 4%" short-sentence figures are unverified folklore —
   use tunable directional gates, not those constants.)
5. **Hard-fail chatbot artifacts:** contentReference, oaicite, oai_citation, turnNsearchN, [cite:,
   grok_card/grok_render, attached_file, ppl-ai-file-upload, utm_source=chatgpt.com, invalid DOIs, and
   mixed curly/straight quotes. Zero-false-positive regex class in Pass C.
6. **Punctuation = author-baseline bands plus an over-sanitization tripwire.** The em dash is a decayed
   authorship signal (GPT-5.1-era suppression shipped Nov 2025; 2026 press rehabilitated it): keep
   ~1 per 300w as a style budget, check comma-slot placement, and flag suspicious absence (zero dashes
   when the author's baseline is nonzero). Tell-free AND texture-free = the humanizer fingerprint.
7. **De-tell with restraint:** minimal-diff, span-local rewrites at constant vocabulary register; never
   fabricate typos, casing errors, or spacing (>90%-detected humanizer signature that also degrades
   trust); on a Pass-C failure generate N candidate rewrites and select by validator score — writer
   models are near-random at judging writing quality (GPT-4o 40.9% vs trained checker 74.3%).
8. **Add presence floors, not just bans:** interactional-metadiscourse floor (zero hedges,
   self-mentions, or attitude markers in long-form = fail — their absence is a measured AI tell);
   information-density gate (concrete specifics per 100w vs exemplar baseline); technical pieces need
   ≥1 admitted tradeoff, ≥2 exact numbers/versions, ≥1 falsifiable opinion.
9. **Ship per-platform validator profiles** — readers judge AI-ness by platform-inappropriateness:
   X post (zero hashtags, no bait closers), X thread (no thread apparatus, 3-5 posts, payoff first),
   LinkedIn (no broetry cadence, no "Agree?"), scripts (cold open, no greetings), newsletter (stance +
   screenshot-able line), announcements (terse; hype register is scam-adjacent in crypto), replies
   (never restate-then-praise).
10. **Extend facts-first into humanization:** every named person/company in an example must exist in
    the Pass-A fact sheet or be marked hypothetical (the "Sarah Chen" problem); replace fake ranges
    ("5 to 10 minutes") with one measured number; for long-form, restyle section-by-section in fresh
    contexts and lint late sections separately — style instructions decay with context length.

## 3. Platform-specific tells and native norms

**X single post.** Tells: any hashtags (Musk banned them from ads 6/2025, told users to stop; credible
crypto/dev operators use zero), engagement bait ("like if you agree" — May-2025 suspensions), "thrilled
to announce", polished hype register (scam-adjacent in crypto). Norms: one opinionated idea whose
natural response is a reply; concrete numbers; lowercase openers permitted (not forced) when the voice
card attests it.

**X thread.** Tells: thread-emoji / point-down-emoji markers, "a thread", "1/23" numbering, cliffhanger
hooks, "RT the first tweet" closers, 20-tweet listicles ("alpha threads" read as shill-farming). Norms:
3-5 posts, core claim in post 1, every post standalone-quotable; threads are ranked as one unit on
completion rate. (Algorithm details SEO-sourced but convergent across 4+ sources.)

**Replies / QTs.** Tell: the aixbt-era bot signature — restate the parent post, add generic praise
("Great breakdown!"), end on an engagement question. Norms: anchor to one specific detail (a number, a
line, a tradeoff); add information or disagreement; never open with praise or summary.

**LinkedIn.** Tells: broetry (one-sentence-per-line cadence + dramatic arc + hashtag block),
"Agree?/Thoughts?/Who's with me?" closers, vulnerability-post genre. LinkedIn shipped an "AI slop"
report category; >50% of long-form was AI by late 2024, so readers assume AI by default. Norms: ≥2
multi-sentence paragraphs; one reusable framework, number, or case per post; ≤2 hashtags (0 preferred).
Caveat: slop still out-engages short-term — engagement is not a quality signal.

**Video scripts.** Shorts: greeting openers are the #1 swipe trigger — ban "Hey guys", "In this video",
"Let's dive in"; line 1 carries the payoff or a concrete number; one deliberately short punch sentence;
≥1 first-person incident detail an aggregator script wouldn't have. Explainers (8-15 min): cold open on
the finished result or failure case; hook + promise + brand sting ≤5-8s; YouTube scores 30-second
retention separately; no pre-roll subscribe/like asks; ban "without further ado" / "welcome back to the
channel". The losing register is the padded middle where the answer arrives at minute 7.

**Email newsletter.** Tells: cross-issue voice flattening, hedge-stacks, disappearance of
screenshot-able risky lines, uniform section geometry, "In today's edition" TOC openers. Norms:
per-send ≥1 falsifiable stance, ≥1 screenshot-able specific line, exact numbers and named sources;
tangents/asides as attested seams. Crypto reference register: "smart friend, plain English" (Milk Road).

**Community announcements (Discord/Telegram).** Tells: siren-emoji or ALL-CAPS hype headers, urgency
pressure ("act fast"), non-critical @everyone pings — all scam-register in crypto, where drainer
channels use the same polished hype. Norms: one-line what-happened; 2-4 lines of concrete detail
(dates, amounts, official links); one action item; ≤6 lines total; one-way signed-broadcast voice.

## 4. Generation-time mitigations and anti-patterns

### What works (evidence-backed)

1. **Facts-first, style-last** — 2026 humanizer master prompts independently converge on the skill's
   exact priority stack: accuracy > clarity > specificity > humanness > style.
2. **Continuation-framed exemplars**: "here are the author's previous posts — write the next one" beats
   "imitate this style" (up to 23.5x style match; 99.9% stylometric agreement). But matched output
   stays ~2x more predictable than human text, so keep the burstiness floor as a separate mechanism.
3. **Verbalized sampling at high-slop-risk spots** (openers, analogies, metaphors, closers): generate 5
   candidates with verbalized probabilities and deliberately pick a low-probability one — 2-3x
   diversity gain; counters typicality-bias mode collapse; model-agnostic.
4. **Best-of-N with external scoring**: on a Pass-C failure, produce several candidate rewrites and
   select by tells+density+diff score, never by the writer model's own preference — LLM self-judgment
   of writing quality is near-random (40.9%), and external-reward selection was preferred by
   professional writers 66% of the time.
5. **Span-local surgical rewrites** (prompt-space analog of antislop backtracking): rewrite only the
   flagged phrase at the same register. Growing "never say X" instruction lists is the prompt
   equivalent of naive token-banning, which breaks at scale (~2,000 patterns) and distorts output.
6. **Target the three measured human-AI gaps**: concreteness (numbers, named tools, lived incidents),
   cultural anchoring (Brazilian/Superteam context), and lexical/structural diversity. Naming them in
   the prompt bridges the gap in >50% of cases — more effective than injecting informality or slang.
7. **Fresh contexts per section for long-form**: style instructions decay with context length ("it goes
   back to the pretty sentences"); restyle section-by-section, lint late sections separately, and
   regenerate any section whose tell density exceeds the piece's earlier sections.
8. **Route-gated severity with a humanity counterweight**: STE-lite numeric caps for procedural blocks
   (≤20-word procedural sentences, ≤25-word descriptive, ≤6-sentence paragraphs, one instruction per
   sentence); off for "anything with a heart"; every severity increase ships with an explicit
   naturalness/seams counter-instruction (Zinsser's fourth principle: humanity).
9. **Weight exemplars toward the informal end for casual briefs** — LLMs measurably imitate structured
   genres better than informal personal writing; expect a lower fidelity floor and require more seams.
10. **Owner-interview mode when the corpus is thin** (<10 on-register samples): a structured voice
    interview generates card material the corpus cannot. (Single-source, practitioner-only — pilot
    before relying on it.)

### Humanization anti-patterns (over-correction that becomes its own tell)

1. **Fabricated imperfections** — typos, forced lowercase, spacing tricks: >90% detected, named
   humanizer artifacts, and readers often prefer clean text anyway. Never do this.
2. **Thesaurus-itis** — out-of-register synonym swaps ("snipping edges" for "cutting corners";
   "utilize" where the author says "use"): the DAMAGE L2 signature. Lint any substituted word markedly
   rarer than the author-corpus norm.
3. **Sentence-chopping** every line into single-clause statements: DAMAGE L3; fluency win-rate 2.67%.
4. **Em-dash panic** — scrubbing punctuation to zero: absence deviates from the author baseline and
   visible sanitization now reads as AI-laundering.
5. **Batch-transforming the whole text** instead of flagged spans — restraint (most of each sentence
   unchanged) is the property that separates good humanization from detectable humanization.
6. **Detector-chasing** as an objective: detectors are adversarially trained and distribution-level
   idiosyncrasies survive paraphrase; tune to human-perceived tells and voice fidelity.
7. **Synonym-inflating the lexicon**: synonyms of overused words are not themselves overused; this only
   manufactures false positives.
8. **Flat-banning without literal-use exemptions** ("underscore" near code, "landscape" in geography):
   false positives are voice damage too.
9. **Uniformly tell-free AND texture-free output**: the combination is itself the humanizer
   fingerprint. Require idiolect-attested seams, never generic roughness.
